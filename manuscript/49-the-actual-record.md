## Forty-nine — The Actual Record

In August there was a leak in the roof of the house.

Nadia and I discovered it on a Sunday morning when I came into the kitchen and found a little puddle on the floor. I thought that the refrigerator had developed a problem. Nadia looked up.

"Oh, hell," she said.

We got a saucepan.

For the next two days I had a considerably greater interest in the weather than I ordinarily had. I also had a rather different conception of the money I'd intended to spend on a new amplifier.

Simon came over to look at the roof.

"Do you know anything about roofs?" I asked.

"No."

"Why did you bring a ladder?"

"Because I don't know anything about your roof in particular."

He went up the ladder.

It turned out that there was some work to be done and that Simon knew somebody who could do it. The man came over the following week. He'd been on the roof for about twenty minutes when Raymond began making a noise.

"Is that yours?" the man shouted.

"No."

"Do you know whose it is?"

"The other Adrian's."

The man leaned over the edge of the roof and looked down at me.

"The other what?"

I told him.

By the time he finished the roof, he'd met the other Adrian, and there was a possibility that the two of them were going to do some work on an old motorcycle.

* * *

I had been home from Europe for about a month when Helen asked me to meet her at the library.

She was preparing a lesson concerning historical documents and wanted to look at a book she hadn't been able to obtain through the school. I told her I'd come and help her look for it.

When I arrived, she was standing by a shelf talking to a librarian. The librarian had already found the book.

"You're late," Helen said.

"Sorry."

"Actually, I've just got here."

She took the book over to the big table.

There was a woman sitting at the other end of it whom I recognized after she'd taken her glasses off.

It was Alma Reyes.

I hadn't seen her for some time. She was wearing a yellow shirt and had a little machine in front of her that she was trying to find out how to use. A man from the library was helping her.

When he went away, I went over.

"Mrs. Reyes?"

She looked up.

"Well, hello."

I sat down.

She was trying to make a calendar for her sister. She'd seen a calendar at somebody's house in which the photographs had been photographs of the people in the family. She thought it was a fairly good idea, although she didn't think there'd been enough photographs of the children.

She showed me some of the photographs she wanted to use.

There was one of Daniel holding a little girl in his arms. There was one of her sister standing beside a car. There was a photograph of the house in Garland.

"That's the front of it," she said.

"I know."

"That's the one Daniel took before we left."

It was a good photograph. The light was coming through the branches of the tree in front of the house.

"Have you been back over there?" I asked.

"Once."

She put the photograph beside the others.

"They painted it white."

"Oh."

"Looks all right."

I asked how she liked the place she'd been living in.

"Fine. The people across the street are very nice."

She thought for a moment.

"I had a better kitchen at the other place."

I nodded.

She looked at the photographs.

"Do you know how to make one of these bigger?"

"No. But I can probably find out."

We found out.

Afterward I asked whether she'd like me to help with the rest of the calendar.

"Well, I've got a lady coming," she said. "She's going to help me with it."

"Okay."

I got up.

"Nice seeing you."

"Nice seeing you, too."

She looked at me again.

"You're the one who helped Daniel with the dresser."

"Yeah."

"That was a heavy dresser."

"It was."

She smiled and went back to her photographs.

* * *

Helen and I went out for lunch.

I told her about the book I was writing. I asked whether she'd be willing to read the part concerning the telephone call she'd made.

"Sure."

I had a copy of it on my computer. I sent it to her.

She read the first few pages while I was getting us something to drink.

When I got back, she said, "Is this how you're going to write the whole thing?"

"More or less."

"Okay."

She looked at the screen.

"Do you remember that conversation?"

"Pretty well."

"Really?"

"Parts of it."

She smiled.

"I don't remember saying that."

"Which thing?"

She pointed at a sentence.

"Oh."

"I might've said it."

"Yeah."

She read the rest of the page.

Then she asked me whether I remembered what she'd been doing before she called me.

"No."

"Well, how would you?"

She told me.

She'd been trying to obtain some information about an appointment from a man who worked in a different department. He'd sent her an extract from a report. There had been a number of people listed in the extract, and several of the entries had struck her as peculiar.

She'd gone through them one at a time.

My entry had been the easiest to investigate. She had my telephone number, and she hadn't been able to find a record of the interview in the places she'd expected to find one.

"What were you going to do if I told you I'd been to the interview?" I asked.

"Go to the next one."

I laughed.

She drank some coffee.

"Do you know what I did after I called you?"

"No."

"I went to buy some feed for Henry."

"Really?"

"Yeah. Leah was going to go out to the stable that evening."

I looked at her.

"That's interesting."

"Is it?"

"Yeah."

"Why?"

I had no very good answer to the question.

I had been regarding the telephone call, for a number of years, as the beginning of a story. It was quite apparent that there were some things it hadn't been the beginning of.

Helen scrolled a little farther down the page.

"Well," she said, "I don't think you need to tell people I went to buy feed."

"Okay."

"Although I did spend an enormous amount of money on that horse."

She laughed.

* * *

Leah was home from college for the summer. She came over to the house one evening with a friend of hers named Lucy.

She was enjoying college. She had decided not to do some of the things she'd been planning to do when she went away, and she was doing several things Helen had never heard of.

"Are you still working with horses?" I asked.

"Yeah."

She told me about a horse named Frank.

"What's he like?"

"He's an asshole."

Helen laughed.

Leah showed me a photograph of Frank. He had his head over a fence, and there was a little bit of grass sticking out of his mouth.

"That's a nice-looking horse," I said.

"Yeah, he's got that going for him."

Lucy was interested in music. She asked whether the guitars in the room at the back of the house belonged to me. I told her they did.

She'd been learning to play for about six months. I asked whether she'd like to play something.

She said she didn't know very much.

I showed her where the little amplifier was.

She played something that I recognized. Then she played something I didn't recognize.

"What's that?" I asked.

"I don't know."

"Did you make it up?"

"Sort of."

"Well, play it again."

She played it again.

We were still playing when Helen came into the room to tell us that she and Nadia had made dinner.

* * *

The following morning, I went through the old interview record.

It had been several years since I'd last looked at the original electronic copy. I'd been using a version Miriam had given me for the lectures, and I'd made a number of notes on it.

The original copy was in a folder containing several other documents. There was a letter from a university informing me that it had decided to proceed with another candidate. There was a description of a research project I'd been trying to persuade somebody to fund. There were some photographs of the apartment.

I opened the first interview record.

There were things about the person in the record that I rather liked. There were also things about him that I would have been quite pleased to see changed.

I wasn't particularly bothered by the fact that some of the things he'd said weren't things I'd ever said.

What interested me was that I had once regarded the differences between us as the most interesting thing about the document.

I read the last page.

**DECLINED.**

I opened the second document.

I read the last question.

*Has it?*

There was no answer to the question.

I took a piece of paper and wrote down something that could be regarded as an answer to it.

Then I crossed it out.

I wrote something else.

That wasn't quite right either.

I began writing an account of some of the things that had happened on the day after I received the document.

Then I wrote about the day before it.

Then I wrote about Ray.

I had thought, when I began writing the book, that I would begin with the telephone call from Helen. But there was a little bit of what had happened before the telephone call that seemed to me to be worth including.

I got up and went to the bedroom.

Nadia was reading.

"Do you remember that blue shirt I used to wear?" I asked.

"Which one?"

"The one I wore when I met you."

"You had about six blue shirts."

"Okay."

She turned a page.

"Why?"

"Never mind."

I went back to the desk.

I had been wondering where to begin the book.

I began with the hole in my sock.
